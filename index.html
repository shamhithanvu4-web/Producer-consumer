Scene 02 · Meet the cast

Three roles on one shared stage

Logline

Two workers operate at different speeds. One creates items; the other processes them. Between them sits a storage area with limited space. When production outruns consumption, the space fills up. When consumption outruns production, it empties. To keep the operation running safely, the workers must learn when to act, when to wait, and how to share access.

        The script behind the scenes

        The simulator runs exactly these two programs. While it runs, the line each worker is on lights up here too.

        Following the simulator

        P

        Producer

        1produce(item)// make the next item
        2wait(empty)// reserve a free slot, or wait
        3wait(mutex)// enter the critical section
        4add item to buffer// buffer[in] = item; in = (in + 1) % N
        5signal(mutex)// leave the critical section
        6signal(full)// one more item is available
        C

        Consumer

        1wait(full)// reserve an item, or wait
        2wait(mutex)// enter the critical section
        3remove item from buffer// item = buffer[out]; out = (out + 1) % N
        4signal(mutex)// leave the critical section
        5signal(empty)// one more slot is free
        6consume(item)// process it outside the lock

        Initial values: empty = N · full = 0 · mutex = 1 (N = 5 by default)

        Synchronization

        decides when a worker can proceed. wait(empty) and wait(full) hold a worker back until the other one makes room or delivers.

        Mutual exclusion

        protects shared-buffer updates. mutex lets exactly one worker inside the critical section at a time.

        Buffer management

        tracks available space and items. Two counters and two indices, in and out, wrap around the slots.

        Questions from the screening

        Why two counters instead of one?
        A semaphore only makes a worker wait when its value is zero. empty reaches zero when the buffer is full, which stops the producer. full reaches zero when the buffer is empty, which stops the consumer. A single counter could only block one side.
        What does the mutex protect?
        The buffer slots and their bookkeeping: the in and out positions and any shared count. Scene 3C shows an update being lost without it, and with several producers or consumers two of them could pick the same slot.
        What happens when the buffer fills or empties?
        The producer blocks in wait(empty) until the consumer’s signal(empty) wakes it, or the consumer blocks in wait(full) until the producer’s signal(full). This is ordinary waiting, not deadlock.
        Why wait for availability before locking?
        A worker that holds the lock while it waits stops the other worker from ever changing the condition it is waiting for. The Blooper take turns that into a visible deadlock.
